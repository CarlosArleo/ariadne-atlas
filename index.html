ariadne — the shape of hope

Around the rim live all the rare diseases, gathered into the families of biology they belong to — the coloured arcs, each disease a faint name and a small radial mark, together drawing the circle. In the interior float the shared origins: the genes, pathways and metabolites where two different diseases secretly meet. The centre is left empty — a quiet void for the deepest, most generic machinery of life, the hubs so universal they explain nothing on their own. The Atlas orbits that void; it never crosses it.

Every thread is a hypothesis. It leaves one disease on the rim, dives inward, and braids through the single shared origin it converges on, before rising to its partner on the far side. Where many hypotheses share the same origin, their threads weave into a bright knot — and that knot is not decoration, it is the biology itself. Each thread is a rare condition reaching toward another: a chance that an insight, a drug, a diagnosis might cross from one child's disease to another's.

How to read it

The rim is every disease, grouped by clinical family (the colour), labelled with its name and a radial tick whose length and brightness grow with how many hypotheses touch it — so recurrent "crossroads" diseases stand out and rare ones fade. Hover a family arc to light all of its connections.

The stars are the shared genes and convergence nodes. Their position carries meaning: the deeper toward the centre a star sits, the more generic it is — a promiscuous hub (like TP53 or ubiquitin) the instrument deliberately distrusts; the closer to the rim, the more specific and precious. Their brightness and size follow discovery quality — the best CQS of any hypothesis meeting there — so specific, well-scored convergences shine while the generic centre stays a hush. Hover any star to see every hypothesis that converges on it; a node is coloured by type (cyan gene, green pathway/process, amber metabolite).

The threads thicken gently with how connected their shared origin is. Hover a discovery in the left list, or on the map, and just that pair lights up: its two diseases named on the rim, its shared gene/pathway/metabolite flared and labelled, and every sibling pair that converges on the same origin glowing softly — the whole family of the same hope.

How it moves when you filter

Nothing here is fixed. The entire picture is computed live from exactly the hypotheses that pass your filters. Change a tier, a fitness floor, a family, a gene, a convergence node — and the stars re-place, the braids re-weave, and the constellation reorganises to reveal what is shared under that lens. That is the whole point: filtering is how the instrument shows you the unknown. By default the Atlas opens on its Exceptional discoveries (CQS 10–13, Tier 3). The right‑hand "Top Convergence Nodes" and "Top Connected Genes" recompute with every filter too — they are the shared origins the current view is resting on.

The geometry is the mathematics made visible. Each thread descends monotonically in radius to its shared origin and back out, so the central void is never crossed — ARIADNE's Poincaré hyperbolic model, where generic scale-free hubs collapse toward the centre (r → 0) and specific, expert-ready convergences are pushed to the boundary rim (r → 1). A star's depth is that specificity; its brightness is its discovery quality (CQS). What you see is not a drawing of the data — it is the data, arranged by its own physics.

Abstract. ARIADNE treats the discovery of shared mechanisms between rare diseases as an evolutionary search over structured, falsifiable hypotheses, scored by a hard-coded fitness function grounded in authoritative biological databases and by the physics of graph topology. What Is ARIADNE?

A genetic algorithm whose organisms are scientific hypotheses. Given two rare diseases, ARIADNE breeds, scores, and culls candidate explanations for how they might share a biological mechanism — surfacing a ranked shortlist for expert review rather than answering from an AI model's memory.

The core idea: breed hypotheses, don't look them up

A population of rough candidate hypotheses varies, each is scored against real biological databases and a panel of checks, the strong ones survive and are bred with small mutations, and the cycle repeats for several generations — good hypotheses improve, nonsense dies out.

Genome vs. genotype

The genome is the 9-slot content of one hypothesis: which two diseases, the type of mechanistic link, which direction the gene fault runs, the step-by-step causal chain, supporting evidence, and — critically — what experiment would prove it wrong.

The genotype is the 8-test scoring panel behind a candidate's Fitness score: topological proximity, evidential pluralism, directionality match, ClinGen tier, phenotype coherence, Bradford-Hill plausibility, population falsification, and novelty/parsimony. Two of these eight can veto a hypothesis outright — e.g. a "causal" variant that turns out to be common in healthy people (gnomAD) is refuted no matter how plausible the story reads.

Why grounding matters

An LLM can write a fluent, confident explanation that is entirely fabricated. ARIADNE's defense is grounding: the AI is used only to propose and phrase hypotheses; hardcoded checks against real databases do the judging. Sensor and judge are always kept separate — the same architectural discipline used across every Aitiopoietic Systems tool.

Where the facts come from

Monarch Knowledge Graph — ARIADNE's ground truth for which genes cause which diseases and symptoms.
ClinGen — expert panels grading gene–disease confidence, Definitive → Refuted.
gnomAD — variant frequency across ~800,000 people; a common "causal" variant auto-refutes a rare-disease claim.
HPO — standard symptom dictionary, used to measure phenotype overlap.
The interactome — the protein–protein interaction map behind the S_ab network-proximity signal.

A computational physics instrument, not a language model

ARIADNE does not guess. It measures disease convergence as a quantifiable property of biological graph topology. Every candidate hypothesis is subjected to 8 physical selection pressures — including network separation, population falsification, and directionality guards — that act as a thermodynamic sieve. The fitness score is not an arbitrary confidence rating; it reflects where a hypothesis sits on a measured free‑energy landscape. Across 14,763 evaluated candidate dyads, the instrument’s output obeys the same non‑equilibrium steady‑state physics as the interactome it explores. The system is not a language model writing plausible biology — it is a computational physics instrument that isolates thermodynamically favoured mechanistic convergences.

ARIADNE Atlas — Filter & Navigation Manual

Learn how to use ARIADNE's clinical, topological, and computational physics controls to uncover hidden disease convergences, eliminate scale-free network artifacts, and surface actionable drug repurposing candidates.

1. Canvas & Visual Navigation

The central concentric diagram maps seed diseases on the inner arc and candidate partner diseases on the outer arc, grouped into 10 clinical pathway families.

Elite Champions Button: Toggles a single-click filter showing only high-confidence Tier 1 and CQS ≥ 7 candidate hypotheses.
Neighborhood Focus Mode: Click any disease node or card to isolate its immediate mechanistic neighborhood across the interactome.
Ribbon Hover & Click: Hover over any ribbon to highlight the connection; click to open the full, expert-ready discovery dossier.

2. Clinical & Quality Filters

Confidence Tier (Tiers 1–5): Ranging from Tier 1 (Definitive / Shared Gene) down to Tier 5 (gnomAD BA1 Refuted / Biologically Incoherent).
Candidate Quality Score (CQS 0–11): An additive readiness metric. Scores $\ge 7$ indicate strong, multi-pathway hypotheses; scores $\ge 9$ are exceptional candidates for expert panels.
Global Cross-Seed Recurrence (Hubs): Filters candidates proposed independently across multiple seed runs ($\ge 3$ seeds). High hubs mark massive "gravity wells" in the interactome (e.g. RASopathy, mTOR, Lysosomal).
Clinical Pathway Family: Categorizes discoveries into 10 domains (RASopathy, Epigenetic, Lysosomal, Channelopathy, Ciliopathy, DNA Repair, Collagen, Mitochondrial, Peroxisomal, Other).

3. Computational Physics & Verification Controls

Poincaré Hyperbolic Boundary (r):
• Specific Boundary Rim ($r \ge 0.50$): Maps specific, hierarchical pathway convergences at the hyperbolic rim. Purges generic scale-free hub shortcuts (*TP53*, Ubiquitin, general inflammation)!
• Central Core ($r < 0.50$): Shows central scale-free network hubs.
Graph Laplacian Spectral Entropy ($S_{VNE}$):
• Tightly Organized Modules ($S_{VNE} \le 1.8$): Isolates low-entropy, highly compartmentalized biological subgraphs with efficient information transfer.
• High Entropy ($S_{VNE} > 1.8$): Shows diffuse or broad pathways.
RANA Literature Verification:
• Supported Only: Filters candidates whose graph assertions are cross-referenced and confirmed by OpenAlex / Semantic Scholar PubMed citations.
• Zero Contradictions: Purges any candidate containing literature-contradicted assertions.
Causal Chain Depth ($N_{nodes}$):
• Deep Multi-Step Chains ($\ge 4$ Nodes): Ensures complete molecular-to-clinical pathways ($Variant \to Gene \to Pathway \to Metabolite \to Phenotype$).

4. Mechanism-by-Mechanism Interactive Search

Instead of searching disease-by-disease, use the bottom-right sidebar cards to navigate by mechanism:

Click Top Connected Genes: Click any gene symbol (e.g., KRAS, B3GLCT, MMUT) to filter the entire Atlas for all diseases converging on that gene.
Click Top Convergence Nodes: Click any GO term, Reactome pathway, or CHEBI metabolite (e.g., GO:0030198 Extracellular Matrix) to isolate that specific biological failure point across all 10,000+ candidates.
