02The filter — what the math actually removes

The "Discovery Gems" gate is five formulas applied at once. Each targets a different failure mode.

Why it matters: this is the whole thesis in one step — the valuable discoveries are not the most frequent nodes (those are generic noise) nor the rarest (those are single-domain validation). They sit in a narrow geometric band: specific enough to reach the rim, cross-domain enough to bridge 2–4 families, novel, and high-quality. The filter finds that band automatically.

03The blind test — does the literature already know?

A discovery filter is only credible if you test it against reality. We asked OpenAlex (~250M papers): for every predicted disease pair, is it already connected in print — and if so, when? Full coverage: bare disease IDs resolved to names via Monarch, nothing skipped.

Two things this proves. Calibration — established links are the largest bucket, and they are real, checkable ones (von Hippel–Lindau ⇄ pheochromocytoma, 1,478 papers; Wiskott–Aldrich ⇄ thrombocytopenia; the FGFR craniosynostoses). It re-derives known biology, so it isn't hallucinating. Discovery — the gem filter lifts genuinely un-studied pairs from a plurality (36%) to a clear majority (54%). The same instrument recovers the known and concentrates the novel.
A second sensor backs this up. A deterministic falsification-specificity grader (script 17) scores how wet-lab-ready each auto-written disproof test is. It honestly finds most raw tests are vague (median 1/6) — which is exactly why the strongest hypotheses were hand-hardened into runnable experiments (stage 07).

06Read it honestly

What this is, and isn't. The literature audit is retrospective: it shows the predictions agree with known biology and concentrate novelty — it does not yet prove ARIADNE would have predicted a link before it was published. The definitive test is a frozen-knowledge re-run (train on pre-2015 facts, score against 2016–2026). No gem is confirmed at the bench yet; each remains a candidate. And the clinical reagents/trials are from an external research audit — verify exact Coriell and trial numbers before citing them. Stating these limits plainly is what makes the rest credible.
