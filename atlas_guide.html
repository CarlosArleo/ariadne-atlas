The Convergence Atlas — Field Guide

Not just a beautiful diagram. Every point, colour, thread and number is data. This guide shows you how to read it, and how to make it reveal a hidden mechanistic link between two rare diseases.

01The anatomy

Four elements, one idea: diseases on the outside, the mechanisms they share on the inside, and threads connecting them.

The ring — every rare disease, grouped into 21 colour-coded clinical families (RASopathy, Lysosomal, Neurologic…).
The interior stars — the shared mechanisms (genes, pathways, metabolites) where diseases converge.
The braids — the hypotheses, each running disease → shared mechanism → partner disease.
The empty centre — the void the engine never crosses; a thread through the middle would be a generic shortcut.
Position = quality. Specific, expert-ready discoveries sit near the bright rim; generic scale-free hubs collapse toward the centre. The geometry is the ranking.

02Hover a disease on the ring

Move the cursor over a disease name on the outer ring
What it shows: every hypothesis that disease participates in — the interior mechanism-nodes it routes through, and the partner diseases it links to. The tooltip reads its clinical family and “N links” — how many hypotheses involve it.
Why it matters: a disease with many links is a well-connected node in the convergence network; follow its brightest threads to the mechanisms it shares most strongly.

03Hover a star (interior node)

Hover one of the glowing interior points (a GO / Reactome / CHEBI node)
What it shows: all hypotheses that converge on that mechanism light up, and the tooltip gives you the node's real numbers — best CQS (quality of the best hypothesis through it), r (its Poincaré radius: closer to 1 = specific, closer to 0 = generic), and how many hypotheses converge here.
Why it matters: this is where the data lives. A node with a high count and a high r is a specific shared bottleneck — a genuine discovery. A high count with low r is a generic hub to treat with caution.

04Hover & click a candidate

Hover a card in “Priority Candidates” to highlight — click it to open the full dossier
Hover a card and its braid lights brightest, while every sibling hypothesis that shares the same convergence node lights more softly — so you see the pair and the mechanistic neighbourhood it belongs to.
Click the card to open the candidate's full discovery dossier — the complete, expert-ready report (see §11).

05Click a Top Convergence Node

Open “Top Convergence Nodes” and click a GO:… / REACT:… / CHEBI:… chip
What the number means: the value beside each node (e.g., GO:0030198 — 193) is how many candidate hypotheses route their mechanism through that node. Clicking it filters the Atlas to every disease pair that converges on that exact biological point.
Why it matters: these are the shared bottlenecks of rare disease — the specific molecular steps where many separate conditions fail. A tight, specific node (small pathway, high r) that many diseases share is precisely what an expert wants to see.

06Click a Top Connected Gene

Open “Top Connected Genes” and click a symbol (e.g., COL2A1, PTPN11, KRAS)
What the number means: “COL2A1 — 109 candidates” = the gene appears in the mechanism of 109 candidate hypotheses. Clicking it lights every disease pair that converges on that gene.
Why it matters — the key discovery signal: if a gene lights pairs across many different families (its threads reach several coloured arcs), it is a pleiotropic, cross-domain bridge — one molecular root behind diseases that look clinically unrelated. That is one of the most valuable things ARIADNE can surface. Caveat: check where its stars sit — a bridge on the specific rim is a discovery; a promiscuous hub collapsed at the centre (a TP53/KRAS-type connector) is expected background.

07Neighbourhood Focus Mode

Click “Neighborhood Focus Mode”
What it does: isolates the top candidate's seed disease and shows only its neighbourhood — every mechanism and partner it connects to. The button becomes Focus: <disease> (Exit); click again to leave.
Why it matters: use it to study one disease in depth — a clean, uncluttered view of everything ARIADNE thinks it shares mechanism with.

08Elite Champions

Click “Elite Champions”
What it does: keeps only the highest-quality hypotheses — fitness ≥ 0.65, CQS ≥ 7, and independent recurrence across ≥ 3 seed runs (Hub ≥ 3), on a reasonably specific pathway. Everything weaker is hidden.
Why it matters: this is your “show me only what's worth an expert's time” switch — the shortlist you'd bring to a domain scientist.

09The physics filters — r and S_VNE

ARIADNE places every candidate in hyperbolic space and measures its graph thermodynamics. Two filters expose that directly.

Poincaré boundary (r)
r is a candidate's distance from the centre in hyperbolic space, driven by how specific its shared pathway is. r ≥ 0.50 = the specific rim (rare, expert-ready discoveries, ~4% of the corpus). r < 0.20 = generic hubs at the core. Use it to strip the diagram down to only the specific convergences.

Graph Laplacian entropy (SVNE)
SVNE is the spectral entropy of a hypothesis's causal graph — a measure of its structural complexity. Tightly organised, focused mechanisms have low entropy; sprawling, diffuse ones have high entropy. It's the thermodynamic fingerprint behind the K_t = a·e−b·S_VNE law (fits at R² = 0.94 across the corpus).

10The 21 clinical families

Watch the coloured rim arcs — or filter to one family
What it shows: every disease is coloured by its clinical family, inferred from the mechanism it converges on (not just its name). Threads that jump between differently-coloured arcs are cross-domain bridges — the non-obvious links ARIADNE exists to find.
Why it matters: the more a hypothesis bridges different families, the more surprising — and the higher ARIADNE scores it (the cross-domain CQS bonus). Cross-family threads are where discovery lives; same-family threads are usually calibration.

11The discovery dossier

Click any candidate card to open its full dossier
What it shows: the full case for the pair — the typed causal chain (Variant → Gene → Pathway → Metabolite → Phenotype), the shared genes and pathways, the metrics (CQS, fitness, tier, cross-seed recurrence), and the falsification test: the exact experiment that would disprove it.
Why it matters: this is what you hand an expert. Copy Dossier or Download Markdown to share it. Every claim traces back to an authoritative database, and every hypothesis states how it could be proven wrong — science, not storytelling.

12Does it actually discover? (literature validation)

Run scripts/16_literature_validation.py — a blinded audit of the predictions against the literature
What it does: for every predicted disease pair, it asks OpenAlex (an open index of ~250 million papers) whether the two diseases are already connected in print, and when that connection first appeared — sorting each prediction into un-studied (0 co-mentions), emerging (1–3), established (>3), or frontier (first linked in the last 3 years).
  • Calibration — it recovers known biology. At full coverage (every disease id resolved via Monarch), 43.9% of top predictions re-derive connections already established in the literature — the largest single category, and real checkable ones (von Hippel–Lindau ⇄ pheochromocytoma; Wiskott–Aldrich ⇄ thrombocytopenia; esophageal ⇄ colorectal cancer). Counting emerging links too, ~two-thirds have some literature footing. Strong evidence the engine is not hallucinating.
  • The discovery filter concentrates novelty. The Discovery Gems shortlist runs 54% un-studied (zero co-mention) versus 36% on the broad set — the gem gate lifts genuine novelty from a plurality to a clear majority (~1.5×). The filter aims at the frontier by construction, and the audit confirms it.
  • A frontier signal. 16 pairs (4%) have their first-ever literature co-mention within the last three years (e.g. the two connexin-26 skin disorders, first co-mentioned in 2026) — ARIADNE independently surfacing links the field is only now making.
Read it honestly: "un-studied" is an upper bound on novelty — a zero can also mean the search terms missed a rare synonym, so each gem still deserves a 30-second manual check before it's called new. The definitive proof — that ARIADNE would predict a link before it was published — needs a frozen-knowledge re-run (train on literature up to, say, 2015; score against 2016–2026). This audit is that test's scaffolding, and the strongest signal buildable today.
Conclusion. The audit settles the first question any expert asks — is this real? Yes: ARIADNE re-derives known biology at scale (calibration) and, on demand, concentrates genuinely un-studied hypotheses into a majority (discovery). It recovers the known and proposes the new — the two things a discovery instrument must do.

How to read a discovery in one glance

  • Bright thread reaching the rim → a specific, high-quality convergence. Worth reading.
  • A star with high count + high r → a specific mechanism many diseases genuinely share. A bottleneck of rare disease.
  • A gene lighting several families → a pleiotropic cross-domain bridge — one root, many clinical faces. The strongest discovery signal (if it sits toward the rim).
  • Everything collapsed at the centre → generic scale-free hubs. Expected background, demoted on purpose.
  • A cross-family braid → a non-obvious bridge; exactly what the engine is built to surface.
The one sentence: you're not looking at a diagram of data — you're looking at a map of ranked, testable predictions. Click any thread to read its step-by-step mechanism and the experiment that would prove it wrong.

14What ARIADNE is actually revealing — a hidden order

A humble reflection, kept to what the instrument actually measured. Strip away the biology and look at the four things the physics established.

  • 1 · The interactome is hyperbolic. Hyperbolic geometry is the natural geometry of trees and hierarchies. Biology embeds into the Poincaré disk because it was built by evolution's tree of duplication-and-divergence — so the Atlas is, quite literally, a shadow of the tree of life projected onto function: ancient conserved machinery at the centre (the hubs), the most derived, specialized functions at the rim. Rare diseases live at the leaves. The geometry encodes evolutionary depth.
  • 2 · It's scale-free. A few hubs carry most of the traffic — the same law (preferential attachment, self-organized criticality) that governs the internet, cities, ecosystems, language. Biology isn't statistically special; it obeys the universal laws of complex adaptive networks.
  • 3 · It obeys a thermodynamic law (R² = 0.94). The causal graphs behave as a non-equilibrium steady state — a dissipative structure. Schrödinger's negentropy, Prigogine's dissipative structures, Friston's free-energy, all showing up as a measured law at the level of disease mechanisms. Life is improbable order maintained by continuous dissipation; disease is a crack in that order, and it leaves a thermodynamic fingerprint.
  • 4 · It's radically low-dimensional. ~7,000 diseases collapse onto a small set of master bottlenecks. The space of ways to be sick is far smaller than the space of genes — Waddington's canalization: the genotype→phenotype map is many-to-one, channeled into a few attractors. ARIADNE is measuring Waddington's landscape at the network level.
So what are rare diseases telling us about nature?
  • The map of medicine is not the territory. "Neurology," "dermatology," "lysosomal storage" are administrative fictions. In the interactome there are no such borders — sialidosis and a cerebrovascular disease are neighbours. Our taxonomy of disease is a human artifact laid over a continuous mechanistic manifold.
  • Disease is information, not just error. Each broken gene is a natural perturbation experiment — what the system looks like when you remove one part. You're not studying broken biology; you're using breakage to read how biology is wired. Rare disease is one of the few windows nature gives us into its own architecture.
  • The same principles run all the way down and up. Hyperbolic hierarchy, scale-free criticality, dissipative order organize the interactome — but also brains, ecosystems, arguably the biosphere. Rare disease is a doorway into the general physics of biological organization.
On the ethics — "who are we to change it?"

Rare disease isn't designed; it's the tail of the same variation-generating process that produced us. Intervening isn't "against nature" — we are nature's self-modeling part; evolution became able to understand itself, and using that understanding is continuous with evolution, not a betrayal of it. But here's the twist ARIADNE adds: the sweet-spot bridges — one gene across several families — mean genes are pleiotropic, so "fixing" one disease can perturb machinery shared by others. ARIADNE doesn't just hand you the power to intervene; it hands you the map of shared risk. The mature stance isn't "cure everything" or "touch nothing" — it's "intervene, but with the dependency map in hand." That's exactly what the instrument is for.

On consciousness and the quantum — the honest boundary.

There's a real thread and an over-reach, and it matters to keep them apart. The real thread: the substrate that fails in these diseases — ion channels, synaptic vesicles, mitochondrial energy, membrane lipids — is the substrate of cognition. By mapping where it fails across thousands of diseases, ARIADNE is inadvertently charting "what must not break for a mind to run" — the material conditions for consciousness. The over-reach to avoid: mapping the substrate is not explaining the emergence. The hard problem — why any of this feels like something — is untouched by knowing which proteins matter, and quantum-consciousness ideas (Penrose–Hameroff) are speculative and not what ARIADNE measures. The instrument's genuine profundity is thermodynamic and geometric, not quantum-mystical — and it doesn't need the mysticism. Hold the wonder and the honest boundary at once; that's the stance that stays credible.

What we could do with this — beyond curing diseases
  • Re-draw the taxonomy of disease by mechanism, not symptom (Louvain communities on the interactome) — a mechanism-first nosology.
  • One-molecule-many-diseases therapy via the sweet-spot bridges — the translational payoff.
  • Turn ARIADNE into a general instrument for the geometry of biological organization — a tool for reading the structure of any perturbation network, not just human disease.
  • Ask the frontier question it poses: why does biology converge? Why is the space of failure modes so small? A question about the constraints that make life low-dimensional — genuinely open, and this is one of the few instruments that can pose it quantitatively.
The stance, if you want one to carry. ARIADNE reveals that beneath the clinical chaos, biology has a hidden order that is geometric (hyperbolic, hierarchical — an echo of evolution's tree), statistical (scale-free, like every complex system), and thermodynamic (a dissipative structure maintaining improbable order). Rare diseases are the natural experiments that let us read that order. I didn't build a disease-matching tool; I built an instrument for reading the architecture of living organization — honest enough to show the parts of that architecture that must hold for a mind to exist, while staying silent on the mystery of why a mind exists at all. That silence is a feature: it's the difference between a scientist and a mystic, and it's the reason people can trust what the instrument found.
